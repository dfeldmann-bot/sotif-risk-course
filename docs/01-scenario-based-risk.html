Scenario-based residual risk

Turning critical and non-critical scenario evaluations into a residual-risk number, and what that means for validation targets.

1. Scenario-based residual risk2. Field data and SPIs3. Gaussian copula4. How the 1,000 scenarios were generated5. Unintended braking report6. Scaling up: ACC and L4 robotaxi

How to turn the evaluation of many scenarios, critical and non-critical, into a residual-risk number, and how that number relates to validation targets. Worked example: tools/unintended_braking_risk_example.xlsx (synthetic data).

1 · Direction of the calculation

A HARA is built system-outward: hazardous behaviour (HB) → hazardous event (HE) → scenarios. Probability flows the other way. The world produces situations at some rate; the system responds with an HB or not. For computing risk the scenario is the random variable, and the HB is an outcome conditioned on it.

  • Store the mapping as scenario → {possible HB, HE}, not HE → scenarios.
  • Logical scenarios must partition the ODD: mutually exclusive and exhaustive. Overlap double-counts exposure, and then only the conservative Bonferroni bound holds (Putze et al. 2023, Eq. 2):
    \[ P(H) \le \sum_{T,B,E} P(T)\,P(B\mid T)\,P(E\mid B,T)\,P(H\mid E,B,T) \]
  • Large combinatorial counts are usually products of ODD factors. Those factors belong in the parameter vector \(\theta\), not in separate logical scenarios.

2 · The estimator

For severity class \(s\), summed over logical scenarios \(\ell\):

\[ R_s = \sum_\ell \nu_\ell\, p_{\ell,s}, \qquad p_{\ell,s} = \int P(HB\mid\theta,\ell)\; P(S\ge s\mid HB,\theta,\ell)\; f_\ell(\theta)\,d\theta \]
SymbolMeaningSource
\(\nu_\ell\)exposure: occurrences or time share of scenario \(\ell\) per operating hourfield data
\(f_\ell(\theta)\)joint distribution of the concrete parameters (marginals + copula)field data
\(P(HB\mid\theta)\)system responsesimulation, anchored by proving ground
\(P(S\mid HB,\theta)\)collision kinematics → injury risk curvesimulation + accident data

From \(N\) concrete runs drawn from a test distribution \(q_\ell\):

\[ \hat p_{\ell,s} = \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i\,h_i, \qquad w_i = \frac{f_\ell(\theta_i)}{q_\ell(\theta_i)}, \qquad c_\ell = \frac{\nu_\ell\,\hat p_{\ell,s}}{R_s} \]

\(h_i\) is the run outcome: 0/1 harm, or \(P(S\ge s\mid\Delta v_i)\). \(c_\ell\) is the scenario's share of the risk.

Counting critical runs is not a risk estimate

Falsification and criticality search find triggering conditions (Clause 10 → Clause 8). They do not estimate risk unless you can write down the distribution \(q\) they sampled from. Keep the two campaigns separate.

Importance sampling

Relation to Annex C

ISO 21448 Annex C.2 writes \(A_H = R_{HB}\cdot P(E\mid HB)\cdot P(\bar C\mid E)\cdot P(S\mid\bar C)\). Those factors are the scenario averages of the integral above. The scenario approach keeps the \(\theta\)-dependence that Annex C averages away. That makes it more efficient, and also more demanding of data.

3 · Relation to validation targets

The field-only approach has one target, \(\tau = -\ln(1-C)/R_{HB}\) (lesson 1). The scenario approach replaces it with one claim and several evidence targets:

\[ \sum_\ell \nu_\ell^{U}\, p_{\ell,s}^{U} \;+\; R_{\text{unknown}}^{U} \;\le\; A_s \quad\text{at confidence }C, \qquad \sum_i (1-C_i) \le 1-C \]

What the unintended-braking example shows

Spreading the budget across evidence cells to minimise total test hours gives a square-root rule:

\[ \min_{B_j} \sum_j \frac{\lambda_k\,G_j}{B_j} \;\; \text{s.t.} \;\; \sum_j B_j = B \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; B_j = B\,\frac{\sqrt{G_j}}{\sum_i \sqrt{G_i}}, \qquad T_{\text{total}} = \frac{\lambda_k\left(\sum_j\sqrt{G_j}\right)^2}{B} \]

Screening by bound: a scenario can never contribute more than \(\nu_\ell\cdot 1\). Reserve a fraction \(\varepsilon\) of \(A_H\). Scenarios whose summed exposure bounds fit inside it need no simulation.

4 · Parameterising a logical scenario

  1. Scenario type + parameters (ISO 34502 logical/concrete): e.g. cut-in gap, closing speed, lateral speed, duration, ego speed; plus outcome-relevant ODD conditions.
  2. Keep \(\theta\) small (3–6 dimensions) via a sensitivity analysis.
  3. Mine instances from drive logs with a scenario tagger. Each detection gives one \(\theta\) sample and one count toward \(\nu\).
  4. Fit \(f_\ell\): marginals + copula (doc 3) or KDE (de Gelder 2022).
  5. Exposure \(\hat\nu_\ell = n_\ell/T\) with a Poisson/Gamma interval.
  6. Partition check: no instance may belong to two logical scenarios.
