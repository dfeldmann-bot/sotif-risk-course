How the 1,000 scenarios were generated

The exact recipe behind the made-up concrete scenarios: distributions, copula sampling, kinematics, importance weights and checks.

1. Scenario-based residual risk2. Field data and SPIs3. Gaussian copula4. How the 1,000 scenarios were generated5. Unintended braking report6. Scaling up: ACC and L4 robotaxi7. Scenario database, ODD coverage and completeness

This documents exactly what tools/ub_sim.py does to produce the sheet Concrete Scenarios. Everything is made up: no field data, no real system, no real simulator. The method is meant to be reused; the numbers are not.

Overview

for each of the 100 logical scenarios ℓ (trigger × speed band × headway class):
  1. draw 2,000 parameter sets θ from the real-world model f_ℓ   → plain Monte Carlo  (reference)
  2. draw    10 parameter sets θ from the proposal q_ℓ           → importance sampling (Concrete Scenarios)
  3. simulate each θ with a 1-D kinematic model                 → collision yes/no, Δv
  4. for the 10 IS runs: w = f_ℓ(θ) / q_ℓ(θ)                     → exact density ratio, incl. copula

100 × 10 = 1,000 concrete scenarios. Reproducible: numpy.random.default_rng(2026). Draw order: Poisson event counts for the 20 evidence cells, then per logical scenario the 2,000 f-runs followed by the 10 q-runs.

Step 2 · Copula sampling

\[ Z = L\varepsilon,\quad U=\Phi(Z),\quad v_e = v_{lo}+U_1(v_{hi}-v_{lo}),\quad h = h_{lo}+s\,(h_{hi}-h_{lo}),\ \ s=\begin{cases}U_2 & (f)\\ 1-\sqrt{1-U_2} & (q)\end{cases} \]
\[ T_r = \exp\!\big(\mu+\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(U_3)\big),\qquad a_f = 3.5 + 4.5\,U_4 \]

Step 3 · Kinematic simulation

One-dimensional, explicit Euler, \(\Delta t = 0.01\) s, horizon 12 s, time from phantom-brake onset:

\[ \dot v_e = -a_e\,\mathbb 1[t \lt t_b,\ v_e \gt 0], \qquad \dot v_f = -a_f\,\mathbb 1[t\ge T_r,\ v_f \gt v_e], \qquad \dot g = -(v_f-v_e) \]

Contact at the first \(g\le 0\): collision = 1, \(\Delta v = v_f-v_e\). Injury \(P(\text{MAIS2+}\mid\Delta v) = 1/\big(1+e^{-(\Delta v-45)/7}\big)\), \(\Delta v\) in km/h (illustrative).

Simplifications: no lateral escape, no brake build-up or jerk, no reaction to the ego's brake release, a single follower, no gradient or friction variation.

Step 4 · Importance weights

\[ w = \frac{f_\ell(\theta)}{q_\ell(\theta)},\qquad \log f(\theta) = \log c_R(z_f) + \sum_i \log f_i(\theta_i),\qquad z_{f,i} = \Phi^{-1}\big(F_i(\theta_i)\big) \]
\[ \widehat{E}\big[\mathbb 1_{\text{coll}}\,P(\text{MAIS2+})\big] = \frac1{10}\sum_{i=1}^{10} w_i\,\mathbb 1_{\text{coll},i}\,P(\text{MAIS2+}\mid\Delta v_i),\qquad \text{ESS} = \frac{(\sum w_i)^2}{\sum w_i^2} \]

What else was made up

  • HB event counts: Poisson(true \(\lambda\) × replay hours) from assumed true rates (grey in the workbook).
  • Exposure shares per factor, combined as a product (independence between the three factors).
  • Replay hours per evidence cell.

Using the method with real data

  1. Replace f's marginals with distributions fitted to your scenario instances, and \(R\) with normal-score or Kendall-\(\tau\) estimates.
  2. Replace simulate() with your simulator. Log \(\theta\), the outcome and \(q(\theta)\) for every run.
  3. Choose q from a pilot run, by shifting marginals toward failure regions or with cross-entropy optimisation. Monitor the ESS.
  4. Keep a pure-f Monte Carlo baseline on a few scenarios as a regression check for the weights.
