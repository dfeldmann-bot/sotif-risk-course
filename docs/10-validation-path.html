From Acceptance Criteria to Test Hours: Budget Allocation and the Role of Importance Sampling in SOTIF Validation

Course note · SOTIF risk course · 30 October 2026
Abstract. The notation follows ISO 21448:2022, Annex C.2.1 [1]. The acceptance criterion \(A_H\) for a harm \(H\) is shared by several hazardous behaviours \(HB_i\). Each behaviour receives a share \(A_{H,i}\), which by Formula (C.2) of [1] becomes a tolerable rate \(R_{HB,i}\) and, by Formula (C.7), a validation target \(\tau_i\). The decomposition into \(R_{HB}\) and the conditional probabilities \(P_{E|HB}\), \(P_{C|E}\), \(P_{S|C}\) is what reduces the required driving. Importance sampling only makes the conditional probabilities cheap to estimate in simulation.

1Acceptance criterion and its decomposition

ISO 21448 decomposes the acceptance criterion of one hazardous behaviour as [1, Formula (C.1), p. 127]

\[ A_H \;=\; R_{HB}\cdot P_{E|HB}\cdot P_{C|E}\cdot P_{S|C} , \]
(1)

with \(R_{HB}\) the rate of the hazardous behaviour, \(P_{E|HB}\) the probability of being in an exposed scenario, \(P_{C|E}\) the probability that the hazardous behaviour is not controllable there, and \(P_{S|C}\) the probability of reaching the severity addressed by \(A_H\). By [1, C.2.1 NOTE 3], \(P_{S|C}\) may be cumulative (“severity at least at a certain degree”). We abbreviate the harm per occurrence of the hazardous behaviour as

\[ P_{H|HB} \;=\; P_{E|HB}\cdot P_{C|E}\cdot P_{S|C} , \qquad A_H = R_{HB}\cdot P_{H|HB}. \]
(2)

2Several hazardous behaviours share one acceptance criterion

When hazardous behaviours \(HB_1,\dots,HB_n\) can each lead to harm \(H\), possibly through several scenarios \(E\), their contributions add [3, Sec. IV-B]:

\[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} R_{HB,i}\cdot P_{H|HB,i} \;\le\; A_H ,\qquad P_{H|HB,i} \;=\; \sum_{E} P_{E|HB,i}\cdot P_{C|E}\cdot P_{S|C}. \]
(3)

Each harm \(H\) (e.g. MAIS2+, MAIS3+, fatal) has its own \(A_H\) and its own inequality (3); one hazardous behaviour appears in all of them with different \(P_{S|C}\).

3Budget split: the share \(A_{H,i}\) of each hazardous behaviour

The criterion is divided into shares \(A_{H,i}\) with \(\sum_i A_{H,i} = A_H\). Formula (C.2) of [1] then gives each behaviour its tolerable rate:

\[ R_{HB,i} \;=\; \frac{A_{H,i}}{P_{H|HB,i}} . \]
(4)

The shares are chosen to minimise the total test effort. With \(c_i\) the cost of one test hour for \(HB_i\) and \(\lambda_i\) the factor of Eq. (6),

\[ \min_{A_{H,1},\dots,A_{H,n}}\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{c_i\,\lambda_i\,P_{H|HB,i}}{A_{H,i}} \qquad\text{subject to}\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{n} A_{H,i} \;=\; A_H . \]
(5a)

The Lagrange condition gives the square-root rule

\[ A_{H,i} \;=\; A_H\,\frac{\sqrt{c_i\,\lambda_i\,P_{H|HB,i}}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sqrt{c_j\,\lambda_j\,P_{H|HB,j}}} . \]
(5b)

For several harms \(H\) the problem is solved over the rates \(R_{HB,i}\) directly, with one constraint (3) per harm; for each behaviour one harm becomes binding. The equal split \(A_{H,i} = A_H/n\) is admissible but not optimal [4, 5].

4Validation target per hazardous behaviour

Formula (C.7) of [1, p. 137], applied to \(R_{HB,i}\) instead of \(A_H\) as in the example of [1, C.2.1], gives the driving or replay time without any occurrence of \(HB_i\):

\[ \tau_i \;=\; \frac{-\ln(1-\alpha)}{R_{HB,i}} . \]
(6a)

Here \(\alpha\) is the confidence, as in [1]. If \(k\) occurrences are observed, \(-\ln(1-\alpha)\) is replaced by the Poisson factor

\[ \tau_i \;=\; \frac{\lambda_i}{R_{HB,i}},\qquad \lambda_i \;=\; \tfrac12\,\chi^2_{2(k+1)}(\alpha),\qquad \lambda_i\big|_{k=0} = -\ln(1-\alpha)\approx 3 \text{ at } \alpha = 95\% . \]
(6b)

Driving targeted at triggering conditions \(j\) (low sun, spray, gantries) is admissible if re-weighted with the exposure share \(\nu_j\) of each condition:

\[ R_{HB,i} \;=\; \sum_j \nu_j\, R_{HB,i,j} . \]
(7)

5Conditional probabilities by simulation with importance sampling

\(P_{C|E}\) and \(P_{S|C}\) are estimated by simulating the scenarios \(E\) with parameters \(\theta\) drawn from a proposal \(q\) that over-samples critical conditions, re-weighted to the real-world density \(f\) [1, C.5, pp. 145–152]:

\[ \hat P \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{f(\theta_k)}{q(\theta_k)}\,h_k, \qquad \mathrm{RSE} \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{sd}\!\big(h\,f/q\big)}{\sqrt{N}\,\hat P} . \]
(8)

\(h_k\) is the outcome of run \(k\): not controlled (collision) for \(P_{C|E}\), or the injury probability from \(\Delta v\) for \(P_{S|C}\) [2]. Non-critical runs enter with their weights. \(N\) is increased until the RSE is acceptable; the upper bound \(\bar P_{H|HB,i}\) enters the verdict.

6Verdict

\[ \sum_{i=1}^{n} \bar R_{HB,i}\cdot \bar P_{H|HB,i} \;\le\; A_H \quad\text{for every harm } H, \qquad \sum_m (1-\alpha_m) \;\le\; 1-\alpha . \]
(9)

Bars denote upper confidence bounds; the second condition is the Bonferroni split of the confidence over all estimated terms [3]. The verdict is on the sum: one behaviour may exceed its share \(A_{H,i}\) if others leave slack.

7Where the reduction comes from

Importance sampling does not reduce driving. The decomposition (1)–(4) does, by the factor \(1/P_{H|HB}\); importance sampling reduces the number of simulation runs needed to estimate \(P_{H|HB}\).

Notation

\(A_H\)
acceptance criterion for harm \(H\) [h\(^{-1}\)] [1]
\(A_{H,i}\)
share of \(A_H\) allocated to hazardous behaviour \(HB_i\) (risk budget)
\(R_{HB,i}\)
tolerable rate of \(HB_i\), Formula (C.2) of [1] [h\(^{-1}\)]
\(P_{E|HB}\)
probability of the exposed scenario given the hazardous behaviour [1]
\(P_{C|E}\)
probability that the hazardous behaviour is not controllable in that scenario [1]
\(P_{S|C}\)
probability of severity (at least) \(S\) given loss of control [1]
\(P_{H|HB,i}\)
harm per occurrence of \(HB_i\), Eq. (3)
\(\tau_i\)
validation target: driving or replay time for \(HB_i\), Formula (C.7) of [1]
\(\alpha\)
confidence, as in [1]
\(\lambda_i\)
\(\tfrac12\chi^2_{2(k+1)}(\alpha)\), Poisson factor; \(-\ln(1-\alpha)\) for \(k=0\)

relative cost of one test hour for \(HB_i\)
\(\nu_j\)
exposure share of triggering condition \(j\)
\(f,\ q\)
