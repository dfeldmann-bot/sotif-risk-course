2 · Statistics to compute

Rates with confidence bounds per ODD stratum and software version:

\[ \lambda^U = \frac{\chi^2_{2(k+1)}(C)}{2T}, \qquad \lambda^U \approx \frac{3}{T}\ \text{for }k=0,\ C=0.95 \]
  • Scenario frequencies and parameter distributions: \(\hat\nu = n/T\) with a Gamma interval. Marginals + copula, with Kendall \(\tau\) per pair, \(\rho=\sin(\pi\tau/2)\), and checks for positive definiteness and tail dependence.
  • Conditional probabilities: HB per triggering-condition occurrence (binomial, Clopper–Pearson \(p^U = B^{-1}(C;\,k+1,\,n-k)\)); collision given HB from surrogates.
  • Extreme value analysis on proximity metrics. Fit a generalised Pareto distribution to the exceedances \(Y-u \mid Y>u\) and extrapolate toward contact:
    \[ P(Y>y) \approx P(Y>u)\left(1+\xi\,\frac{y-u}{\sigma}\right)^{-1/\xi}, \qquad Y = -\text{TTC}_{\min} \]
  • Discovery rate of unknown scenarios (Clause 11). Good–Turing estimate of the probability that the next scenario is of an unseen type: \(\hat P_{\text{new}} \approx n_1/N\) (types seen exactly once ÷ all occurrences).
  • Representativeness: \(\chi^2\) or Wasserstein divergence between tested and target ODD distributions per stratum.
  • Change detection: CUSUM or sequential tests on rates; exact conditional Poisson test for software-version rate ratios.

3 · Safety performance indicators

UL 4600: an SPI is “a metric supported by evidence that uses a threshold comparison to condition a claim in a safety case”, and “relying solely on lagging indicators is not acceptable” (quoted in Ratiu et al. 2024). Koopman: a KPI asks how well do we drive; an SPI asks how often are we potentially unsafe.

Each SPI needs four things: (1) the safety-case claim it conditions; (2) the metric and its data source; (3) a threshold derived from the risk budget; (4) the response when it is breached. Responses: update the safety case, restrict operations (speed, geofence), update the system, or ground the fleet.
