SOTIF · validation targets

Who may contribute how much

Acceptance criteria are set per severity class. Hazardous events share them, and hazardous behaviours trigger the events. The budget split decides how much harm each behaviour is allowed to cause. That allowance is its validation target.

The map

Read it right to left. Each acceptance criterion (AC) has one budget. Every hazardous event (HE) passes a fraction of its occurrences to each AC, and every hazardous behaviour (HB) produces each HE at some rate.

w HE occurrences per unit HB rate: exposure of the situation × P(not controlled)
π share of an HE's outcomes that reach a severity class, from its Δv distribution and an injury-risk curve
r the HB rate you test for. Its allowed maximum is the validation target

The one rule

For every acceptance criterion, add up the harm arriving from all behaviours through all events. The sum must stay within the budget.

Σb rb · Wb,s ≤ As   with   Wb,s = Σe wb,e · πe,s   for every AC s

The events drop out into W. You need them to compute the weights and for traceability, but the budget is split over the behaviours, because the behaviours are what you test.

The split, solved

Minimising total test hours under all three rules at once gives one validation target per behaviour. Zero-failure targets at 95 % confidence, so hours = 3 / r.

How to use it on a full HARA

  1. Fix a few severity classes per harm type. Typically MAIS2+, MAIS3+ and fatal, cumulative, each with a limit per hour. Subtract the reserve for unknown triggering conditions.
  2. Give every HE its severity fractions π. From the Δv distribution in simulation and an injury-risk curve. One HE gets one fraction per class, not a single severity.
  3. Estimate w for every HB–HE pair. Exposure of the operational situation × P(not controlled). For 24 HBs and 160 HEs this is a sparse table.
  4. Solve for the targets. Minimise Σ cb·λk,b / rb subject to the one rule. The output is rb per HB and which class binds it.
  5. Freeze, test, sum. Fix the targets before confirmatory testing. Pass if the sum of upper bounds stays within every criterion; one HB may overrun its target if another has slack.

Choices that change the answer

Severity fractions per HE

Each HE uses budget in proportion to the harm it really causes. Needs simulated Δv and an injury curve.

Worst severity per HE

ISO 26262 habit. Every occurrence counts as severe, which wastes most budget on frequent, mostly mild situations.

One weighted measure

For example fatality-equivalents. A single constraint, but the weights are a value judgement and many mild cases can hide a few fatal ones.

Fixed share per HE

Allowed if your process wants a number per safety goal. Adds constraints, so total testing can only grow.

Keep one convention: cumulative classes (fatal also counts toward MAIS3+ and MAIS2+) or disjoint ones. Mixing them double-counts or loses harm.
